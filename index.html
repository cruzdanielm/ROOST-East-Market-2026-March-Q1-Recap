The one-line read

East Market is beating both its budget and its prior year — and by a widening margin. Q1 Rental Revenue ran +17.1% ahead of plan and +21.7% ahead of Q1 2025, the occupancy premium is the widest on record (MPI 136.2 YTD vs comp-set +2.3% YoY Occ growth vs East Market's +13.0%), and the rate premium has moved back into positive territory (ARI 107.8; Q1 ADR $204 vs $189 LY, +$15 / +8.0%). March compounded all three signals into the first positive NOI month of 2026 ($30K vs $2K LY) and a +21.1% RevPAR YoY print that is the widest single-month index on record. The mix shifted materially toward medical and long-stay demand — Medical segment revenue grew from 7.7% of Q1 PY to 26.9%, and 30+ night monthly stays rose from 33.4% to 41.1% of the book on the arrival of CHOP, Jefferson, Guthrie Clinic, and HUP as new or expanded named accounts (top-15 named book +$215K / +130% YoY). Heading into Q2 the watch items are (1) converting the occupancy advantage into an ADR premium the leased cost base actually needs to cover the $135K/month rent load, (2) insulating the medical book against its new concentration, and (3) defending the ratings lead (Revinate 4.63 composite #1 of 6, NPS 70.21 in elite territory) while recovering the -15-pt Service and -9-pt Value sentiment moves that sit beneath the headline score.

What drove the quarter

Performance highlights

  • Occupancy leadership. Q1 Occ 85.9% vs 77.0% budget (+8.9 pts) and vs comp set 63.1% — MPI 136.2 YTD. March Occ 91.6% at MPI 130.7, rank #1 of 6 in the set every month.
  • March NOI flip. March delivered $30K of positive NOI against a budgeted ($11K) loss — a $41K favorable move in a single month, the strongest NOI print since stabilization.
  • Medical-segment surge. Medical revenue (CT MED + TR NEG MED) grew to $203K (26.9% of Q1) from $49K (7.7%) a year ago — CHOP, Jefferson Hospital, The Guthrie Clinic, and HUP expanded materially and closed the YoY gap by themselves.
  • Long-stay compression. 30+ night monthly-stay revenue rose to $311K (41.1% of Q1) from $211K (33.4%) — +$100K / +47% YoY on the strength of medical, government, and relocation demand.

Strategic areas of focus

Three items to manage into Q2

Push rate on the occupancy floor. East Market runs MPI 136 with ARI only 108 — a signal that compression is arriving at a rate point the market is willing to pay more for. Moving ARI toward 115 without losing meaningful MPI is the single largest NOI lever against the fixed rent base.

Balance the medical concentration. Medical segment now carries 26.9% of revenue (vs 7.7% PY). That's a powerful demand engine, but it also concentrates risk around CHOP, Jefferson, Guthrie Clinic, and HUP. Broadening the non-medical 30+ night book — Churchill, Astrid, Nomad, Sirva — re-diversifies the monthly-stay layer.

Convert reputation into direct-channel share — while recovering Service sentiment. Revinate 4.63 (#1 of 6), Cleanliness 4.95, Service 4.86, TA Popularity Index 0.93 (#1 of 6), NPS 70.21 (+40.4% YoY, elite tier). The property is the rating leader of its competitive set — but Service sentiment fell -15 pts and Value -9 pts YoY, and review/survey volume is thinning. Protecting the asset means rebuilding the arrival experience and the response base; monetizing it means widget placement in confirmation/post-stay flows to take pressure off commissions (+$4K over plan in Q1).

What moved the month

Three drivers behind the $41K beat vs budget and $28K beat vs LY

2 · Rate held against both benchmarks. ADR $221 vs $205 plan (+$16, +7.8%) vs $202 LY (+$19, +9.4%). Medical and long-stay demand arrived without forcing rate discounting. East Market's +9.4% YoY ADR outpaced the comp set's +0.6% YoY — pricing power, not compression noise. The rate variance alone produced ~$22K of the Room Revenue beat vs budget and ~$26K of the lift vs LY. Ancillary revenue (pet, parking, cancellation) added $12K YoY on longer-stay guest behavior.

3 · Flow-through held despite expense pressure. Total OpEx +6.8% vs plan and +16.0% vs LY against Net Revenue +23.8% vs plan and +27.7% vs LY — a 17.0-point leverage gap to budget and 11.7-point gap to LY. Budget and LY tell the same story this month — and the YoY win is cleaner. March is ahead of both reference points on revenue, rate, occupancy, and NOI. Where they diverge is magnitude: the YoY revenue lift (+27.7%) is larger than the vs-plan beat (+23.8%); the YoY NOI improvement ($28K) is smaller than the vs-plan beat ($41K) because March 2025 was already marginally positive ($2K) rather than a budgeted loss ($11K). Both views support the same read — March represents a true operating step-change, not a favorable-comp accounting artifact.
